Dear Professor Zeldovich,

I hope this message finds you well. I am the founder of **Secura Academy**, a
free, open-source learning platform for systems security, built as a WordPress
plugin and theme and published on GitHub
(`github.com/boucheriechefimane-cmd/secura-academy`). Students work through
hands-on rooms with **real, isolated virtual machines** (Docker), with
progressive unlocking, medals, scores and timed exams. The interface is
available in **French, Arabic and English**; the platform is **free for
learners**, and its code is MIT-licensed.

**What we have already done, and why we are writing.** We imported the quiz
questions and the **official solution notes** from your 6.858 *Computer Systems
Security* course (Fall 2014, published on MIT OpenCourseWare under
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): 132 questions in total, including 115 written exam problems
that we present to students as "problems from a university exam", with MIT's
official corrections displayed after the student writes their own answer. Every
question carries its source, course, year and licence; the room shows the
attribution, and our derived content is redistributed under the same
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms. We did not write or rewrite any of these questions.

**What we would like to ask for.** The material published on your course sites
is outstanding teaching material, and it is exactly what our students need. We
would like to ask for **written permission to reuse it** in our platform, and
we would like to do so under the following conditions, which we propose and
are ready to adjust:

- every question carries a visible attribution: course, number, year, MIT,
  and a link to the original PDF;
- MIT's official corrections are shown as such, with MIT's wording preserved
  verbatim;
- no endorsement is implied: we state that MIT does not sponsor or endorse the
  platform;
- questions are never sold on their own; what is offered to learners is the
  surrounding learning experience (rooms, isolated labs, mentoring, an optional
  paid certificate of completion);
- we are happy to publish the platform's licence, source code and import
  pipeline so that the provenance of every question is auditable.

**The material we have in mind** (all of it already public, all of it with
official solutions):

| Course | Material | Links |
|---|---|---|
| 6.5660 / 6.858 (2009–2026) | **34 past quizzes with official solutions** (Quiz 1 and Quiz 2 for Fall 2009–2015, Spring 2017–2020, Spring 2022–2024, Spring 2026) | <https://css.csail.mit.edu/6.5660/2026/quiz.html> |
| 6.1600 (2021–2024 + practice) | **10 past exams with official solutions** (midterms and finals 2021–2023, quizzes 2024) | <https://61600.csail.mit.edu/exams/> |
| 6.1600 (2026) | Quiz review handouts **with solutions** | <https://61600.csail.mit.edu/2026/> |
| 6.5660 (2026) | Per-lecture "Question" bank | <https://css.csail.mit.edu/6.5660/2026/questions.html> |

This represents roughly **three to four hundred additional questions with
official corrections**, which would let us offer our students genuinely
university-grade material in French and Arabic, and would fill the exam
reservoirs that are still too small for one student per question.

**Our questions.**

1. Would MIT be willing to authorise this reuse, and under which terms?
2. Is there a MIT Learn / Open Learning process that fits a free, multilingual
   project, and would you point us to it?
3. Is there an attribution wording you would prefer?
4. Are the official corrections permitted to be displayed to students after
   they write their own answer, as we do today?
5. If the answer is "not from me", who else should we contact?

We would be glad to share the platform, the licence, and the import pipeline in
advance, and to adapt to whatever conditions you set.

Thank you for your time, and thank you for the course — it is the reference
our whole pedagogical path is built on.
